## 3. CD-23 resolution

Constitutional Debt CD-23 (recorded in Session 2720 §8.5) identified an evidence threshold gap on PLAYBOOK-10.13.1 as originally authored. The rule was classified `[GR]` Governance Rule, requiring E1|E2 + E6 evidence, but was cited only against E3 (`docs/research/platform/playbook_authoring_session_2719.md` §8).

### 3.1 Re-evaluation of the recommended solution

The mission required re-evaluation of the previously-recommended solution (reclassify PLAYBOOK-10.13.1 from `[GR]` to `[EP]`) in light of the now-authored stub chapters.

**Solution A (reclassify [GR] → [EP]):** The [EP] Engineering Principle threshold requires min 1 evidence source. The single E3 citation (`playbook_authoring_session_2719.md` §8) satisfies. The rule's substance — "The Constitutional Debt Register MUST be maintained as a running record of items intentionally deferred from ratified Playbook content" — expresses an engineering principle about how deferred items are tracked, rather than a specific governance rule about the amendment process itself.

**Solution B (add citations to satisfy [GR] threshold):** [GR] requires E1|E2 + E6. Available candidates:
- E2 RATIFICATION_20260708_0199_CYCLE_1_CLOSEOUT does not directly evidence Register discipline; it does not name the Register concept.
- No E6 session handoff exists that records ratified Register discipline; the Register is a concept introduced by Session 2719 (an E3 research output), not a ratified session handoff.

Solution B does not have qualifying evidence.

**Solution C (defer §10.13 to a later Playbook version):** Would leave Chapter 10 incomplete at v0.1 and would require the Debt Register to operate without codified discipline through v0.1 ratification and beyond.

**Solution A remains the strongest solution.** Stub-chapter authoring did not surface any new evidence that would change the outcome. The Register discipline is architecturally a principle, not a procedural governance rule.

### 3.2 Applied correction

**Correction applied:** PLAYBOOK-10.13.1 is reclassified from `[GR]` Governance Rule to `[EP]` Engineering Principle. The rule text is unchanged. The evidence citation is unchanged.

**Reclassified rule (as it stands after this correction):**

> **[EP] PLAYBOOK-10.13.1** The Constitutional Debt Register MUST be maintained as a running record of items intentionally deferred from ratified Playbook content. Each debt entry MUST include a unique identifier of the form `CD-NN`, a description, a rationale for deferral, an earliest version at which the item is eligible for resolution, and a blocking status. [E3: `docs/research/platform/playbook_authoring_session_2719.md` §8 (Constitutional Debt Register established)]

**Justification:** The rule expresses an engineering principle about record-keeping for deferred items rather than a governance rule about the amendment process. The `[EP]` threshold (min 1 evidence source) is satisfied by the E3 citation.

**Nature of the correction:** This is a pre-ratification draft correction, not a Playbook amendment. Chapter 10 §10.4.1 explicitly prohibits reclassification via PATCH; §10.5.2 prohibits modification of existing rules via MINOR; §10.6 permits reclassification only via MAJOR. Because v0.1 has not yet been ratified, the reclassification is a draft-author correction rather than an amendment governed by Chapter 10 discipline. After v0.1 ratification, any similar correction to a subsequent version would follow Chapter 10 discipline as a MAJOR amendment.

**CD-23 status:** RESOLVED.

## 6. Rigby Constitutional Audit preparation package

This package is self-contained. Rigby (or any independent constitutional auditor) MAY use this package as the sole input for the audit alongside the Playbook body and the frozen evidence manifest.

### 6.1 Audit scope

The audit covers:

- **Chapters 0 through 10 draft content** (eleven chapters).
- **165 total rules** as enumerated in §5.1 above.
- **Frontmatter of each chapter** (chapter ID, purpose, scope, statement classes present, rule ID range, evidence anchor).
- **Cross-references** between and within chapters.
- **Evidence citations** on every normative rule.

The audit does NOT cover:

- The frozen evidence manifest itself (its content is settled).
- The research chain (Sessions 2708–2715) that produced the architecture (settled).
- The pre-existing constitutional ecosystem (Canon Registry, ADR corpus, peer OSes, SYSTEM_OWNER.md, DOC_LIFECYCLE.md) — these are cited by the Playbook but not audited by the Playbook audit.
- Runtime behavior of the platform — the audit is of the Playbook document, not of platform runtime state.

### 6.2 Audit objectives

The auditor MUST determine for each of the 165 rules:

1. **Evidence traceability:** does the rule cite at least one source in the frozen evidence manifest, and does the citation resolve to substrate that substantively supports the rule?
2. **Classification defensibility:** is the rule's statement-class marker (`[AC]`, `[EP]`, `[GR]`, `[OR]`, `[IP]`, `[RS]`, `[RP]`, `[RC]`, `[DR]`, or `[RM]`) defensible given the rule's substance?
3. **Threshold satisfaction:** does the rule's cited evidence satisfy the admission threshold for its statement class per Chapter Provenance Classification §Evidence Admission Standard?
4. **RFC 2119 usage:** does the rule contain exactly one capitalized RFC 2119 keyword, per Chapter Preamble §Interpretation of Normative Language?
5. **Cross-reference resolution:** does every cross-reference the rule invokes resolve to authored content (or is appropriately marked as pending)?

The auditor MUST determine for the Playbook as a whole:

6. **Constitutional consistency:** do any two rules contradict each other?
7. **Duplicate detection:** do any two rules duplicate the same substance (as distinct from intentional layering)?
8. **Chapter boundary discipline:** does each chapter's content match its declared scope?
9. **Terminology consistency:** are terms used consistently across chapters, with definitions in the chapter that introduces them?
10. **Provenance discipline:** does every citation identify its content provenance class (verified primary evidence, verified repository or runtime fact, verified quoted source, historical reconstruction, or engineering conclusion) or is it identifiable from context?

### 6.3 Audit exclusions

The auditor MUST NOT:

- Propose new rules or new chapters.
- Propose new architectural concepts.
- Modify the frozen evidence manifest.
- Propose changes to the pre-existing constitutional ecosystem (Canon Registry, ADR corpus, peer OSes, SYSTEM_OWNER.md, DOC_LIFECYCLE.md).
- Perform new architectural research.
- Author replacement rules (though the auditor MAY suggest reclassification per §6.5).

Rigby's mission is to determine whether the Playbook accurately expresses already-ratified architectural truth, not to redesign the truth.

### 6.4 Expected outputs

The audit produces:

1. **Batch-organized SIGN report** with a minimum of three batches for a v0.1 initial audit:
   - Batch 1: Chapters 0 and 1 (constitutional context).
   - Batch 2: Chapter 6 (provenance discipline).
   - Batch 3: Chapter 10 (amendment discipline).
   - Optional Batch 4: stub chapters 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9 (stub coverage).

3. **Rule-level table** identifying each rule reviewed, its verdict, and any finding.
4. **Debt Register additions** for items surfaced by the audit that are architecturally deferrable.
5. **Ratification-readiness recommendation:** either "recommended to proceed with System Owner Directive" or "return for correction with itemized F-BLOCKING findings."

### 6.5 Blocking findings definition

An F-BLOCKING finding is one that MUST be resolved before v0.1 can be ratified. Categories:

- **Broken citation:** a rule cites a source that does not exist, does not resolve at the cited location, or does not substantively support the rule.
- **Undefined term used normatively:** a rule uses a term of art that has no definition anywhere in the Playbook body.
- **Contradiction:** two rules produce mutually exclusive requirements in the same scope.
- **Threshold violation:** a rule's cited evidence does not satisfy the admission threshold for its statement class.
- **Statement-class violation:** a rule's classification is indefensible given its substance.
- **Multiple RFC 2119 keywords:** a normative sentence contains more than one capitalized RFC 2119 keyword.
- **Missing rule identifier:** a normative sentence lacks a `PLAYBOOK-N.M.K` identifier.
- **Circular reference:** a chain of cross-references forms a loop that does not resolve to substrate.

Non-blocking findings are those that reduce quality but do not compromise constitutional integrity. Examples: readability improvements, opportunity to relocate a rule (deferred to MINOR amendment), an unused extension point, an inconsistent use of the `> **Commentary:**` convention.

Informational findings are those that the auditor records for context but that require no action.

### 6.6 Constitutional success criteria

The v0.1 draft is deemed a stable constitutional foundation when the audit produces:

- **Every one of the 165 rules traceable** to at least one source in the frozen evidence manifest.
- **Every cross-reference resolves** to authored content or is appropriately marked pending.
- **Every rule's evidence satisfies** its statement-class admission threshold.
- **Zero unaddressed F-BLOCKING findings** at the close of the audit and any subsequent correction pass.
- **The overall discipline is coherent** across chapters (no cross-chapter contradictions; no undefined terminology; no circular references).

Success criteria are met when the audit report explicitly recommends the draft be ratified.
